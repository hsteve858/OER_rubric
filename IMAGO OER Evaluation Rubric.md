# OER Evaluation Rubric

Oct 27, 2026 · @HA Stevies

## How to use this rubric

This rubric rates the potential quality of each open educational resource (OER) IMAGO curates, using nine criteria scored from 0 to 3. It adapts the Achieve OER rubrics and adds a licensing criterion.

- **Review the smallest meaningful unit.** That may be one lesson, video, reading or activity, or a full unit. For a resource with many lessons, decide first whether to review all of them, only the essential ones, or a sample (for example every third or fifth lesson).
- **Score each rubric on its own.** A weak score on one criterion should not pull down another.
- **Rate potential, not proven results.** You are judging how well the resource should work in a learning setting, not how it performed.
- **Use N/A when a rubric does not fit.** N/A is not a low score. For example, Rubric IV (assessment) does not apply to a resource with no assessment component.
- **Rubric VIII is a yes/no checklist,** not a quality score. Only reviewers who feel qualified to judge accessibility should complete it.

| Score | Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Superior | Meets every condition listed for the top level |
| 2 | Strong | Solid, with one or two specific gaps |
| 1 | Limited | Useful idea or content, but needs significant supplementing |
| 0 | Very weak / none | Confusing, erroneous or unsound; not useful as presented |
| N/A | Not applicable | The rubric does not fit this resource's intended purpose |

## Rubrics I to IV: alignment, explanation, teacher support, assessment

### Rubric I. Degree of alignment to standards

Use when the resource has proposed alignments to standards or outcomes. Judge both the content and the performance expectations of each proposed standard. IMAGO standards or outcomes: \[add here\].

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | Both are true: every content and performance expectation in the standard is fully addressed, and those expectations are the focus of the resource, not a peripheral part. |
| 2 Strong | Either minor elements of the standard are not addressed, or the standard aligns to only a minor part of the resource. |
| 1 Limited | A significant part of the standard's content or performance expectations is missing, but the resource stays faithful to the part it does cover (for example, a standard on counting to 1000 where the resource stops at 500). |
| 0 Very weak | The resource does not match the standard, or matches only minimally important aspects of it. |
| N/A | No standards are proposed for alignment (for example, a raw data set). |

### Rubric II. Quality of explanation of the subject matter

Use when the resource is meant to explain content to learners. For teacher-facing resources, rate the explanation of the content, not the planning instructions.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | All are true: the information is comprehensive and effective enough for the target audience to understand the content; important related concepts are connected (for example, multi-digit addition is tied to place value); no added explanation or materials are needed; and the main ideas are clearly identified for the learner. |
| 2 Strong | The content is explained so that skills, procedures, concepts or information are understandable, but connections among related concepts are missing (for example, the procedure is taught without place value). |
| 1 Limited | The content is explained correctly but only briefly. It is too thin for a first-time learner and would mostly serve as review. |
| 0 Very weak | The explanations are confusing or contain errors, and are unlikely to contribute to understanding. |
| N/A | The resource is not designed to explain content (for example, a formula sheet or a map). |

### Rubric III. Utility of materials designed to support teaching

Use when the resource is meant to help teachers plan or present content. Judge usefulness for the majority of teachers at the intended level.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | All are true: materials are comprehensive and easy to understand and use; suggestions are given for a variety of learners, such as common-error tips and precursor skills; every component is provided and works as described (accurate time estimates, complete materials lists, sensible explanations); and for larger units, materials support a mix of approaches such as direct instruction, group work and investigations. |
| 2 Strong | Materials are comprehensive and easy to use, but either there are no suggestions for a variety of learners, or some core components (such as directions) are underdeveloped. |
| 1 Limited | The resource has a useful idea for an important topic, but important elements are missing or do not work as intended (unclear directions, missing or inadequate practice). Teachers would need to supplement it. |
| 0 Very weak | The resource is confusing, contains errors or is missing important elements, so it is not useful despite its intent to support teachers. |
| N/A | The resource is not designed to support teacher planning or presentation. |

### Rubric IV. Quality of assessment

Use when the resource is designed to find out what students know before, during or after instruction. Rate a set of assessment items as one unit.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | All are true: everything assessed clearly aligns to the intended content and performance expectations; nothing outside the intended scope is assessed unless labeled as extension; the most important expectations get appropriate weight; the assessment modes (selected response, short or long constructed response, group work) require real demonstration of the skill; and difficulty comes from the content and cognitive demand, not from unrelated barriers such as overly complex vocabulary in word problems. |
| 2 Strong | All intended content and performance expectations are assessed, but the modes used do not consistently let students demonstrate proficiency. |
| 1 Limited | Some intended expectations are assessed, but important ones are omitted or students lack opportunities to show proficiency. |
| 0 Very weak | The assessment has significant errors, misses important content or skills, is confusing to students or is otherwise unsound. |
| N/A | The resource has no intended assessment component. |

## Rubrics V to VII: interactivity, practice, deeper learning

### Rubric V. Quality of technological interactivity

Use when the resource has a technology-based component that responds to the individual user. This rates interactivity, not technology in general, and not interaction between students.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | All are true: the resource responds to learner input in a way that individualizes the experience (it adapts, or gives the user real control); the interactive element is purposeful and tied to learning; it is well designed and easy to use; and it works flawlessly on the intended platform. |
| 2 Strong | The interactive feature is purposeful, well designed, easy to use and reliable, but does not individualize the experience. Motivational features such as points or levels are acceptable if they do not detract from learning. |
| 1 Limited | The interactive element is unrelated to the subject matter and may detract from learning. It may raise motivation slightly but does little to support understanding. |
| 0 Very weak | Interactive features are poorly conceived or built: they fail to work as intended, distract the user or waste the user's time. |
| N/A | There is no interactive technological element (for example, the only interaction is opening a PDF). |

### Rubric VI. Quality of instructional and practice exercises

Use when the resource includes exercises meant to build skills and knowledge. Rate a set of exercises as one unit. For complex tasks, one or two rich exercises can count as enough.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | All are true: there are more exercises than the average student needs for mastery; exercises are clearly written and backed by accurate answer keys or scoring guidance where applicable; and there is variety in exercise type or format suited to the skills, including formats that let learners integrate skills on complex tasks. |
| 2 Strong | There are enough well-written exercises for mastery, with accurate answer keys or scoring guidance, but little variety in type or format. |
| 1 Limited | There are some exercises but too few for mastery, there are no answer keys, and there is no variation in type or format. |
| 0 Very weak | The exercises do not support mastery, contain errors or are otherwise unsound. |
| N/A | The resource has no opportunities to practice targeted skills. |

### Rubric VII. Opportunities for deeper learning

Use when the resource is designed to engage learners in at least one of these skills: think critically and solve complex problems; work collaboratively; communicate effectively; learn how to learn; reason abstractly; construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of others; apply knowledge and skills to real-world situations; construct, use or analyze models.

| Score | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 3 Superior | All are true: at least three of the skills above are required; the range of cognitive demand suits and supports the material; and appropriate scaffolding and direction are provided. |
| 2 Strong | One or two of the skills above are included (for example, a complex problem that requires abstract reasoning). |
| 1 Limited | One skill is included but clear guidance on how to engage it is missing (for example, learners are told to collaborate but the process and product are unclear). |
| 0 Very weak | The resource appears designed to offer deeper learning but is not useful as presented, for example because of poorly formed problems or unclear directions. |
| N/A | The resource is not designed to offer deeper learning, even if it could be adapted to. |
